Firewall and exposure inbound and outbound

Inbound is read from Shodan's InternetDB, a free lookup of what its crawlers last saw on an address: ports, service names, CVEs. It is a record, not a live scan, and it is the same record an attacker reads first. Outbound is tested from this server: whether DNS to a public resolver is answered by the router instead, whether DNS-over-HTTPS and SMB leave the network, and whether IPv6 reaches the LAN.

04
DNS can bypass the Pi-hole
Plain DNS to 8.8.8.8 and DNS-over-HTTPS to 1.1.1.1 both succeed from the LAN. A device with a hard-coded resolver, or a browser with its own DoH, is not filtered. The usual fix is a router rule that redirects port 53 to the Pi-hole and blocks the known DoH endpoints, or NAT that forces 53 to the resolver.
Why it matters. Network-wide filtering only covers devices that use the network's resolver; without a rule, an app that brings its own DNS walks past it. More

What is and is not scanned

  • This product never port-scans your public address itself; it asks InternetDB, which publishes what its own crawlers saw.
  • The outbound tests send one packet each to public resolvers and one probe host; nothing is fetched from an advertising or malware domain.
  • The IPv6 probe needs an outside vantage; the mockup assumes a small reachability endpoint this product runs, the same way the DNS self-checks are run by the resolvers.
